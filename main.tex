\begin{abstract}
Text-to-motion generation has mostly been built for one body: the joint count,
joint order and pose parameterisation of a single human skeleton are baked into the architecture.
We present \method{}, a text-conditioned motion diffusion transformer that serves hundreds of
heterogeneous animal skeletons with one set of weights. Three design choices make this possible. First,
\rep{} is a per-joint representation into which any rigged animation can be converted from its kinematic
tree, rest pose and motion alone. Every (rig, joint, channel) cell is normalised by its own statistics, so
that rigs of 34 to 102 joints share one network input without a template, retargeting or inverse
kinematics. Second, the skeleton enters as a prompt: its rest pose is prepended to the sequence as a clean
frame, and natural-language descriptions of its joints together with a graph-distance attention bias tell
the network what each joint is. Nothing in the model is specific to a skeleton, so a rig it has never seen
is served by the same forward pass once its per-cell statistics are supplied. Third, a grouped
flow-matching objective whose channel weights are calibrated from measured group energies rather than
hand-tuned ensures that a channel group's share of the gradient does not depend on the number of cells it
spans. Trained on a captioned corpus of production animal rigs and scored by a skeleton-aware text--motion
evaluator, \method{} reaches R@1 \num{0.867}, where the real clips score \num{0.904}, and an FID of
\num{0.005}. Motion for a rig outside the library is generated by the same forward pass with no training
of any kind, and a per-rig low-rank adapter on roughly ten clips brings its rotation and position
channels into agreement on bodies far outside the training distribution.
\end{abstract}
