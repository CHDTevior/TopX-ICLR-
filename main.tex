\begin{abstract}
Text-to-motion generation has mostly been built for one body: the joint count,
joint order and pose parameterisation of a single human skeleton are baked into the architecture.
We present \method{}, a text-conditioned motion diffusion transformer that serves hundreds of
heterogeneous animal skeletons with one set of weights. Three ingredients make this work. \rep{} is
a per-joint representation that any rigged animation can be converted to from its kinematic tree,
rest pose and motion alone, with every (rig, joint, channel) cell normalised by its own statistics,
so that rigs of 34 to 102 joints share one network input without a template, retargeting or
inverse kinematics. The skeleton enters as a prompt: its rest pose is
prepended to the sequence as a clean frame, and natural-language descriptions of its joints together
with a graph-distance attention bias tell the network what each joint is; nothing in the model is
specific to a skeleton, so a rig it has never seen is served by the same forward pass once its
statistics are supplied. And a grouped flow-matching objective whose channel weights are calibrated
from measured group energies rather than hand-tuned keeps root, body and contact channels balanced
across rigs of very different size. Trained on a captioned corpus of production animal rigs and scored
by a skeleton-aware text--motion evaluator, it reaches R@1 \num{0.867} against \num{0.904} for the real
clips, at FID \num{0.005}. A rig outside the library generates through the same forward pass with no
training of any kind, and a per-rig low-rank adapter on eight to twenty-six clips brings its rotation
and position channels into agreement on bodies far outside the training distribution.
\end{abstract}
