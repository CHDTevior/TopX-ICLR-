\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

We ask four questions. (1) How well does one set of weights serve hundreds of heterogeneous
skeletons on text-conditioned generation (\S\ref{sec:exp:main})? (2) How far does the model
extend to a rig it has never seen, with no training and with about ten clips
(\S\ref{sec:exp:extend})? (3) Which capabilities does it combine that released
systems provide separately, and what does the released arbitrary-topology model produce on the same rigs
(\S\ref{sec:exp:compare})? (4) Which ingredients matter
(\S\ref{sec:exp:ablation})?

\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:exp:setup}

\paragraph{Library and splits.} The training library is a \num{311}-rig animal corpus in \rep{}
(\num{77{,}894} clips, \num{6.18}M frames at 30\,fps; deformation bones only, 34--102 joints per rig, median 64,
\num{108} distinct kinematic trees); cells are standardised with the standard-deviation floor
$\epsilon_\sigma{=}0.05$ (\S\ref{sec:method:rep}); validation holds \num{3{,}899} clips, \num{5}\% of every rig's
(App.~\ref{app:split}). Rigs outside the library come from
the Truebones zoo~\citep{truebones}: \num{986} clips over \num{66} rigs converted to \rep{} with the same
pipeline (\num{802} captioned), each rig split $80/20$ into train and validation clips.

\paragraph{Models.} The main model has \num{303}M parameters (14 blocks, width 896, 14 heads);
a \num{36}M pilot (8 blocks, width 384, 6 heads) shares every other setting and is used for
ablations. Both train at global batch 128 with the calibrated objective, the main model for
\num{290} epochs at $1.5{\times}10^{-4}$ and the pilot for 400 at $2{\times}10^{-4}$, with a
4{,}000-step warm-up and a half-cosine decay over 40 epochs to $1\%$ of the peak.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Generation uses 20 Euler steps and text guidance $s{=}2$ everywhere
(\S\ref{sec:method:sampling}), at the length of the real clip (at most 240 frames), from the epoch-289
checkpoint; Table~\ref{tab:main} is the mean of five sampling seeds, every other library table uses seed 42,
and the Truebones arms of \S\ref{sec:exp:extend} use seed 7 and the epoch-214 snapshot that initialised the
adapters. Retrieval metrics follow \S\ref{sec:method:eval}: text-to-motion R-precision at $K{=}1,2,3$ in
pools of 64 formed by chunking the dataset order (a retrieved motion is correct if it is the caption's own
clip), the FID on the evaluator's $\ell_2$-normalised embeddings and the text--motion matching score, each
next to the evaluator's score on the real clips of the same pools (a reference, not a bound). The evaluator
scores the sampled channels; re-scoring the same samples with the position and velocity channels recomputed from
the forward kinematics of the predicted rotations --- the animation the rig plays --- moves R@1 by $+0.001$ at
\num{303}M and $-0.003$ at \num{36}M (App.~\ref{app:metrics}). Geometry is measured in world space on that
deployable output. The \emph{FK--pose gap} is the mean distance, in the rig's mean bone length, between the
forward-kinematised and the directly predicted joint positions: a consistency of the two output channels, not a
distance to the target. On Truebones rigs, for which no skeleton-aware evaluator exists, articulation, jitter and root speed
are reported as ratios to the rig's real clips (App.~\ref{app:metrics}).

\subsection{One model for 311 skeletons}\label{sec:exp:main}

training, so this measures the generation of held-out clips on known rigs; rigs outside the library are

model reaches R@1 \num{0.867} against \num{0.904} for the real clips scored by the same evaluator

\subsection{Extending the model to rigs it has never seen}\label{sec:exp:extend}

\paragraph{Zero training and per-rig LoRA.} We take four Truebones rigs that differ from the library in body
plan and joint count -- Buffalo (30 joints), Gazelle (29), Dragon (95, a flying rig) and Spider (68) -- and
compare three arms on the same held-out clips of each: (a) the library model with the new rig's per-cell
statistics as its only rig-specific input
(\emph{zero training}); (b) a per-rig low-rank adapter trained on the rig's 8--26 training clips
(\emph{LoRA}, \S\ref{sec:method:lora}); (c) a \num{36}M model of the same recipe trained on all \num{802}
captioned Truebones clips, evaluation clips included (\emph{TB-only}). Table~\ref{tab:lora} gives the
FK--pose gap before and after adaptation next to the articulation and jitter of the same sampled output: eight to
twenty-six clips bring the gap from $0.57$--$4.5$ bone lengths to $0.23$--$0.35$, most on the two rigs farthest
from the library, and the seen-clip reference sits at $0.08$--$0.21$. The jitter is gone within 50--100 epochs
and the gap plateaus by 100--150 (Dragon: 450, App.~\ref{app:extend_more}).
Statistics computed from the adaptation clips alone give the same picture: the gap collapses to $0.25$--$0.36$
and the jitter excess goes (Table~\ref{tab:supportonly}).

\subsection{Relation to prior work}\label{sec:exp:compare}

\paragraph{Capabilities.} The two capabilities this paper joins are available separately in released
systems (Table~\ref{tab:open}): those that take the kinematic tree at inference are not text-conditioned, and
the text-conditioned animal generators are built around one fixed skeleton. \method{} returns rotations and a
world-space root trajectory the rig plays as generated, and measures that geometry against ground truth
(Table~\ref{tab:geom}). Table~\ref{tab:capability} adds the systems whose code is not public.


\paragraph{AnyTop as an in-distribution reference.} The released AnyTop weights trained on the whole
Truebones zoo, including the four rigs of Table~\ref{tab:lora}, so its per-rig geometry
(App.~\ref{app:anytop}) is a reference for what a model trained on these rigs produces.

\subsection{Ablations}\label{sec:exp:ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:simple} removes the ingredients of \S\ref{sec:method:model} and \S\ref{sec:method:objective}
one at a time from the \num{36}M pilot and adds the flat construction fixed-skeleton generators are brought to
variable skeletons with (App.~\ref{app:flat}; same corpus, objective, group weights, schedule and budget, \num{5}\%
more parameters). The calibrated group
weights carry \num{0.036}--\num{0.043} of R@1; the joint descriptions none on their own; removing the descriptions
together with the geodesic and direction biases and the structural features costs a further
\num{0.029}--\num{0.033}; and flattening the joint axis costs \num{0.192}--\num{0.201} and multiplies the FID by
\num{3.7}.

Table~\ref{tab:geom} measures what the representation buys on the deployable output, together with the two
world-coordinate loss terms it makes writable and the 13-channel layout cannot carry. The 13-channel AnyTop
layout derived from the same clips is within a point of R@1 of \rep{} at every matched epoch, in its favour,
and \num{0.008} apart once its objective and partition are matched (App.~\ref{app:ablation_notes},
Table~\ref{tab:repcontent}): retrieval does not resolve the layout. Geometry does. The two output channels of a
\rep{} sample agree to \num{0.38} bone lengths against \num{1.45} at \num{36}M (\num{0.24} at \num{303}M); its
leaf joints articulate, where the 13-channel layout has no rotation slot for them and the conversion fills it
from the parent; its feet slide less while flagged in contact; and its root travels within \num{4}\% of the
data's displacement, against \num{22}\% further for the layout that integrates a root velocity. Per-cell mean
and standard deviation are worth about two points of R@1 and \num{22}--\num{30}\% of the FID over a per-rig
scale alone (Table~\ref{tab:rep}), and at 200 epochs the \num{36}M, \num{116}M and \num{303}M models reach
R@1 \num{0.821}, \num{0.843} and \num{0.857} (Table~\ref{tab:size}).
